Useful compilations

  • @StellaAthena created the Common LLM Settings spreadsheet which can be a super-useful resource when you're about to embark on a new LLM training - as it tells you how many known LLM trainings were created.

  • A few years back I started compiling information on which dtype the models were trained in - it only contains a handful of models but if you're doing a research on dtypes it can still be useful. I was using this information to try and write a model pretraining dtype auto-detection and here is a related float16 vs bfloat16 numerical properties comparison.

Publicly available training LLM/VLM logbooks

(A VLM is a vision-language model: an LLM that also takes images as input, see Multimodal LLMs.)

Logbooks, chronicles, and technical reports of training LLMs/VLMs are among the best sources to learn from about dealing with training instabilities, infrastructure and scaling challenges, and choosing good hyper parameters. The earlier entries here are logbooks/chronicles kept during a training run; increasingly the same hard-won lessons appear as dedicated sections of technical reports - typically titled something like Infrastructure, Scaling, and Efficiency or Engineering Challenges and Solutions, and often covering reliability and operational challenges that rarely make it into a model card.

If you know of a public LLM/VLM training logbook, or a technical report with such a section, that is not on this list please kindly let me know via an Issue or add it via a PR. Thank you!

The listing is in no particular order other than being grouped by the year.

2021

  • BigScience pre-BLOOM 108B training experiments (2021): chronicles | the full spec and discussions (backup: 1 | 2)

2022

  • BigScience BLOOM-176B (2022): chronicles-prequel | chronicles | the full spec and discussions (backup: 1 | 2 | 3)

  • Meta OPT-175B (2022): logbook | Video (backup: 1)

  • THUDM GLM-130B (2022): en logbook | Mandarin version (backup: 1 | 2)

2023

  • HuggingFace IDEFICS-80B multimodal (Flamingo repro) (2023): Learning log | Training Chronicles (backup: 1 | 2)

  • BloombergGPT 50B LLM - section C in BloombergGPT: A Large Language Model for Finance

2024

  • MegaScale: Scaling Large Language Model Training to More Than 10,000 GPUs - the paper covers various training issues and their resolution - albeit on models that are proprietary yet just as instructional/useful.

  • Imbue's From bare metal to a 70B model: infrastructure set-up and scripts very detailed technical post covers many training-related issues that they had to overcome while training a proprietary 70B-param model.

  • Meta's The Llama 3 Herd of Models devotes §3.3 Infrastructure, Scaling, and Efficiency to the training stack, including §3.3.4 Reliability and Operational Challenges - notably how environmental factors (diurnal temperature swings, and the effect of tens of thousands of GPUs' fluctuating power draw on the datacenter grid) perturbed training throughput, a dimension I haven't seen documented in other reports.

2025

  • Project Apertus (ETH Zürich, EPFL, CSCS) - Apertus v1 Technical Report: Democratizing Open and Compliant LLMs for Global Language Environments - a fully open 8B/70B suite trained on 15T tokens across 1800+ languages using up to 4096 GPUs on CSCS's Alps. Its Infrastructure, Scaling, and Efficiency and Engineering Challenges and Solutions sections (~5 pages) read like a logbook of the run.

2026

  • Supercharging Olmo-core for Efficient and Scalable MoE Training - practical recommendations for training large scale MoE models in 2026!
  • Marin 535B-A23B MoE live - each short entry goes to a details GitHub Issue where you can read the details. Finished trainings retrospectives: 8B, 32B ("535B-A23B" reads as 535B total parameters of which 23B are active per token, the usual way of naming a Mixture of Experts model)
  • Project Apertus - Apertus v1.5 Technical Report: From Open Text Models to Open Multimodal Reasoners - continued multimodal pretraining of the Apertus 8B/70B models; like v1 it includes Infrastructure, Scaling, and Efficiency and Engineering Challenges and Solutions sections (~5 pages).

Hardware setup logbooks

  • Imbue published a detailed log of how they have set up a 512-node IB-fat-tree cluster (an InfiniBand network wired as a fat tree) and made it to work: From bare metal to a 70B model: infrastructure set-up and scripts, they also open-sourced the cluster tooling they created in the process.

  • SemiAnalysis published a great detailed writeup about what it takes to set up a Neocloud cluster (a neocloud is a GPU-focused cloud provider such as CoreWeave or Lambda, as opposed to the big general-purpose clouds).
